# What a JAKESJAM far truer to the axioms would look like

**What this is.** A thought experiment, not a directive. It asks: if you pushed
`design-axioms.md` to its logical end, what game falls out — and where does that
diverge from where JAKESJAM is heading? It's a north-star tension to reason
against, not a plan to execute. Nothing here says "rip out the class system";
it says "here is the axiom-maximal pole, so you can choose where on the line to
stand — deliberately."

---

## The one thing under every deviation

Read the deviations audit end to end and a single pattern surfaces. D1 (too many
loops), D2 (a catalog that collapses), D3 (unbraked engines), AX.1's "fold the
meters," the bot fixity, first-blood's rich-get-richer — they are all the **same
move made repeatedly: reaching for depth by ADDITION.** Another system, another
meter, another loop, each one *hoping* to add depth.

The axioms bet the opposite way. They are, almost to a line, a case for depth by
**SUBTRACTION**:

- **A5** — keep ≤4 major loops; more is noise.
- **A7** — value scales with *combinations of few orthogonal parts*, not with the
  count of parts (discrete infinity).
- *"95% of a game's fun is avoiding elementary errors and tuning, not innovation"*
  — the genius is the invisible plumbing, not the new noun.
- *"Less is more — abstraction increases transferability; subtraction often beats
  addition."*
- *"The whole is genuinely more than the sum"* — emergence comes from few loops
  interacting, and you cannot read its quality off the rule count; you must *run
  it*, and the curve from rules to richness is non-linear (often downward).

So the honest framing of the whole audit isn't "fix these three bugs." It's:
**JAKESJAM is currently a game that seeks depth by adding systems; the axioms
describe a game that seeks depth by subtracting them and deepening the
combinations of what's left.** Those are two different games. Both can be great.
But every system you add spends loop-budget the axioms say is nearly gone.

*(Aside, said plainly because it matters: your own recorded failure mode is "too
many structures — subtract, don't build." The game-design theory arrives at the
same verdict from a completely independent direction. When your instinct and the
canon agree, that's signal.)*

## The redesign, six moves

### 1. One economy, learned once (A4, A5, A14)
Today a fighter tracks: `abilityCharge` + (soon) four class resources
(mana/energy/Kindling/Devotion) + shield charge + stolen-fangs locks + per-slot
cooldowns. **Truer:** one participation meter, 0–100, filled by fighting, spent by
everything — it *is* the Emission charge and the ability fuel and the class
resource. One bar the newcomer learns in one fight. This dissolves D1/AX.1 by
construction rather than by patching. The reward it pays (fill by dealing/taking
damage) already steers toward the fight (A14) — so make it the *only* reward
channel and let its meaning compound.

### 2. Four loops, each named and braked (A3, A5)
Choose the major loops on purpose instead of accreting them:
- **Fight** — fill the meter by fighting, spend it to win fights. Brake: it drains;
  you can't bank forever.
- **Escalate** — round-end draft grows the build. Brake: catch-up weights (*already
  the model — A3 done right; copy it, don't reinvent it*).
- **Chaos** — the per-round modifier. Self-braking (rerolls, symmetric). *Already
  A6-perfect.*
- **Finish** — sudden-death as the *finisher* (A3: positive feedback's right job is
  ending a decided game fast).

That's four. **Cut or fold the rest:** first-blood is unbraked within-round positive
feedback (A3 leak) — cut it or fold its "reward aggression" intent into the meter's
fill rate. Pity-boss is a *second* catch-up mechanism doing what the draft weights
already do — one brake, not two (A5). Resonance is a *fifth* timing loop — fold it
into the meter as an implicit combo flourish, not a stopwatch the player runs.

### 3. One build language, refracted through the classes (A7)
The Six Axes are *already the right idea* — orthogonal, compose-never-cancel,
discrete-infinity, "your gun is a sentence." **The four classes KEEP — they are the
axioms' strongest application, not a violation.** Four orthogonal *ways to play*
(project / cut / hold-the-board / entangle) is discrete-infinity done right: the class
is the **lens** (the cast frame / verb — a nova through a blade is a spin-slash; through
a board a quake) that makes the *one* build language expressive across four fantasies.
That is additive- and subtraction-depth at once — the sweet spot.

**What subtracts is the machinery hanging off the classes, not the classes:** the
axiom-true version resists building a *second, magnitude-ranked content matrix* beside
the one orthogonal language. The pick-3-of-10 catalog is where that risk lives (D2 — it
can collapse to one meta rack) — so the discipline is to keep those abilities *orthogonal
words in the same language* (each a genuine different-in-kind choice, brake-carrying,
sharing the one economy), not a separate bar of distinct mechanics balanced in isolation.
Keep the four lenses; keep the abilities; hold the line that they refract *one* system
instead of spawning a parallel one. The ~120 identities the card pool already promises
come from combination, not from count.

### 4. The fighters are always the loudest thing on screen (A1, A13, "VFX never hides hitboxes")
This isn't a polish pass; it's a doctrine. The footage review found the backdrop
out-shouting the rigs and remote players snapping-then-freezing — both are the same
failure: the *read* lost to decoration and to jitter. **Truer:** the backdrop is
scenery (dim, subordinate); every mechanic reads *at its site* the instant it
happens (the six-axes legibility law, extended to everything); netcode smoothness is
a *correctness* constraint, not a nice-to-have. A game whose combat you can't read
has no depth to reveal, however many systems sit under it.

### 5. Reveal complexity at the newcomer's pace (A12, A15)
A URL-clicker's round 1 should surface **one** loop: shoot, hit, feel it. The draft,
the abilities, chaos, the meter — revealed as they escalate (A15: the gap is the
game; hand out the next layer exactly when the player is ready to close it). The
current game front-loads its whole system stack onto second five. Truer: round 1 is
almost the old game; round five is unrecognizable — *for the player's understanding*,
not just their build.

### 6. Difficulty falls out of resources (A11)
Progress is already a resource (score, health, streak, meter). So DDA is a *query*,
not a system: bots read the human's state and adapt — down for the drowning
newcomer, up for the smurf — instead of a fixed `slideTier`. Free adaptive
difficulty, no bespoke difficulty engine.

## What this is NOT saying

- **Not** "the class overhaul is wrong." It's a real, coherent bet — *addition-depth*:
  more identities, more to master, more content surface. Many beloved games make it.
- **Not** "ship less content." Subtraction-depth ships *more expressed identities*
  (discrete infinity) from *fewer underlying parts* — the card pool's own "~120
  identities from 31 cards" is exactly this.
- **Not** a demand. It's the far pole of a spectrum so the middle is a *choice*.

## The actual decision

The question the axioms put to JAKESJAM is not "which of these is a bug" but:

> **Which depth are you buying — addition (more systems, more mastery ceiling, more
> to learn) or subtraction (fewer systems, more emergent combination, more
> learnable)? And does each system you add pay its loop-budget rent, or is it
> hoping depth will emerge from accumulation the axioms say it won't?**

A JAKESJAM far truer to the axioms is **one economy, four braked loops, one
orthogonal build language, the fighters always loudest, complexity revealed at the
player's pace, difficulty read off resources** — and the courage to reach depth by
taking things away. That's not necessarily the game to build. It's the mirror to
build against.
