# JAKESJAM — Marketing Copy

Source-of-truth copy for portal/store listings and outreach. Pulls the
one-sentence pitch from `game-design-document.md` §0 — keep these in sync
if that pitch ever changes.

## Why the "no pay-to-win, ever" line is here explicitly

Competitor review-sentiment research (2026-07-14): across every tracked
title in JAKESJAM's genre, review sentiment splits cleanly on monetization
model, not on genre or budget —

| Model | Titles | Review range |
|---|---|---|
| Premium, no IAP | Stick Fight, ROUNDS, Duck Game, Gang Beasts, Rivals of Aether | 85–97% positive |
| F2P with real monetization (battle pass / character unlocks) | Brawlhalla, MultiVersus | 76–80% positive |

Not a close call. JAKESJAM's existing cosmetics-only stance already sits
on the right side of that line — this should be a *stated* line in every
listing and pitch, not an implicit assumption a player has to discover.
(See `game-design-document.md`'s monetization-guardrails note: cosmetic
loot/gacha presentation is fine to explore later, selling power never is.)

## Portal listing description

Use for CrazyGames/Poki and similar submission forms.

> **JAKESJAM** is a browser-first 2D multiplayer arena shooter — fast
> movement, punchy weapons, and round-to-round upgrade drafting turn
> simple gunfights into escalating, physics-driven chaos. Dash through
> enemy fire, chain wall-jumps into a bash that ends the fight, and draft
> a new card every round to build a weapon nobody else in the lobby has.
> No pay-to-win, ever — every upgrade is earned in the match, and
> cosmetics never touch power.

## Outreach kit pitch paragraph

Use for cold outreach, press kits, portal partner pitches.

> JAKESJAM is a browser-first arena shooter built for the physics-chaos
> subgenre streamers already love (Stick Fight, Gang Beasts territory) —
> dash-bash collisions and wall-jump momentum produce unscripted,
> unrepeatable knockback moments that generate clips nobody can predict,
> not scripted combos. Round-to-round card drafting means every match
> escalates into a different weapon build, so no two clips look the same.
> It's cosmetics-only, no pay-to-win, ever — the same stance the
> highest-reviewed titles in this genre (85–97% positive) already share,
> and the one MultiVersus/Brawlhalla-style real-monetization titles don't
> (76–80% positive).
