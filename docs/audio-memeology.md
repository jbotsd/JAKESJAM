# Audio Memeology — the grammar of meme SFX

Meme sounds are parts of speech. Each has a register, a placement law, and
a mix law. The SFX marks the joke's structure; it is never the joke.

## The lexicon

| Sound | Function | Placement law | Register |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vine boom** | emphasis detonation | ON the stressed syllable of the punchline — never after it | universal, but max 1–2 per short or it's slop |
| **Crickets** | the comedy vacuum | enters 150–300ms AFTER the line (the slot where laughter should be); needs ≥1.2s of real dead air; being cut off mid-chirp by the next line is the second joke | awkward-silence reactions |
| **MLG airhorn** | ironic hype certification | layered WITH the hype moment, under it, never solo-foreground | self-aware — it mocks hype while delivering it |
| **Error / denied buzzer** | bureaucratic refusal | on or a hair after the refusing word | institutional comedy only |
| **Record scratch** | interruption / freeze | requires a visual freeze-frame to earn it | "yep, that's me" archetype |
| **Metal pipe** | chaos non-sequitur | over physical comedy / ragdoll only | absurdist |
| **Bruh** | disappointment reaction | 100–300ms post-fail, low in mix | reaction slot |
| **Sad trombone** | institutional failure | post-punchline | reads boomer unless double-ironic |
| **Taco bell bong** | scene transition | on the cut itself | current-gen punctuation |

## The four laws

1. **Structure, not substance** — the sound marks setup→punch→react. If
   removing the SFX kills the joke, the joke was never there.
2. **Two timing classes** — EMPHASIS sounds (boom, buzzer, horn) land ON
   the stressed syllable. REACTION sounds (crickets, bruh, trombone) land
   in the laugh slot, 100–300ms after the line ends.
3. **Felt, not heard** — −12 to −18 dB under the VO. Exception: vacuum
   sounds (crickets) own their silence at near-full presence.
4. **Rarity is value** — each sound once per short. The second boom
   halves the first.

## The double-tap

Hard-cutting a ringing SFX with the next line's first syllable is itself
a joke (the interrupt). Crickets especially: let them establish (~1.2s),
then murder them with the next sentence.
