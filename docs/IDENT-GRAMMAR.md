# IDENT-GRAMMAR — The Design Grammar of Legendary Studio Idents

Research dossier for the 27.9s JAKESJAM boot ident.
Aesthetic frame: vessel instrument geometry (concentric dial rings, inscribed crystal DIAMOND, radial ticks, monad spark) in bone-ivory + dim gold over obsidian, teal as the single energy accent. Track is the entire soundtrack — no foley, no UI blips.

> **SYMBOLISM CONSTRAINT (owner's hard line, 2026-07 — applies to ALL JAKESJAM visuals, not just the ident).**
> The banned thing is the **composition**, not the elements: **no Eye-of-Providence read** (an eye/pupil element under or inside a triangle), **no triangle capping ring geometry around a focal center**, and **no hexagram/pentagram** (including accidental composites — the shader once mirrored the SVG triangle and the two read as a hexagram; that class of bug is a content violation, not just a render bug).
> Eyes/apertures **alone** are fine (the visor-seam "aperture, not a face" motif is core vessel grammar) and triangles **alone** are fine elsewhere (projectiles, beams). Do not reintroduce a triangle as the seal's centerpiece over the ring stack. The centerpiece is the inscribed crystal **diamond** (rotated square, vertices on the cardinal axes) — avionics/crystal-munitions grammar per `visual-language-gnostic-vessel.md`.

Track structure (fixed):

| Phase | Time | Musical character |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet intro | 0 – 4.8s | low, sparse, anticipatory |
| DROP | 4.8s | first hit |
| Mid-energy build | 5 – 14s | rhythmic construction energy |
| High | 14 – 19s | escalation |
| Peak | 19 – 26s | full energy |
| Resolve | 26 – 27.9s | decay / landing |

Spoken line: "Jakes Jam — by Intrepid Development" (timing measured separately; placement guidance in §Synthesis).

## Part 1 — Per-ident breakdowns

### 1. Valve (Half-Life 2 era — "valve guy")

**What it is.** A single sepia photograph of a heavy-set man with a valve wheel embedded in his eye socket (HL1/HL2 era, "Open your eyes") or the back of his skull (Orange Box onward, "Open your mind"). HL2 toned the color down and added subtle "trembling" at the frame edges. The 2011+ version adds one event: the man slowly begins turning his head, then snaps back.

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~15–20s, effectively ONE beat. Fade in from black (~2s) → hold the still image with an imperceptible slow zoom/tremble (~12–15s) → fade out (~2s). No cuts. In the 2011 version there is exactly one mid-phrase event (the head turn) at roughly the 2/3 point.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** No hits at all. A continuous low industrial drone with faint metallic shimmer — closer to Source-engine ambience than to music. The audio never "lands" on anything; the absence of sync is the point. Visual change is sub-threshold (zoom, tremble), so the viewer's attention has nowhere to go except *into* the image.
- **(c) The one thing:** Single-image inevitability. One disturbing, unexplainable photograph, held far longer than any commercial instinct would allow. Notably, when Valve finally added motion (the head turn), it read as a jump-scare *precisely because* two decades of stillness had trained the audience — stillness banks tension you can spend later.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive = confidence. Nothing animates because nothing needs to. The craft is in the photography (they street-cast real "heavy-set, normal" Seattle men rather than agency models — Ray Ueno/Gabe Newell, mid-90s) and in the restraint. The cheap version of this would add a lens flare, a logo swoosh, a whoosh SFX — every addition would subtract.

Sources: [Combine OverWiki — Mr. Valve](https://combineoverwiki.net/wiki/Mr._Valve), [Half-Life Fandom — Mr. Valve](https://half-life.fandom.com/wiki/Mr._Valve), [Kotaku — Valve Intros, Ranked](https://kotaku.com/valve-intros-ranked-1842908433), [AVID — Valve Corporation](https://www.avid.wiki/Valve_Corporation).

### 2. THQ (1990s–2000s metallic ident)

**What it is.** CGI chrome: the T·H·Q letterforms and red bar fly in *in pieces* over a scratched gray-metal background and assemble onto a black shield; background drops away; "PRESENTS" spreads out below.

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~5–7s, three beats: (1) pieces fly in, (2) assembly/impact, (3) shine + "PRESENTS".
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Textbook hit-mapping. Synth whooshes track the flying pieces; a male announcer barks "T-H-Q!" with each syllable landing **as the matching letter arrives**; a metallic *thud* lands exactly when the red bar slams the shield; an electric buzz accompanies the shine. Every audio event has a visual owner; nothing lands "near" a hit — always ON it.
- **(c) The one thing:** Assembly-on-syllable. The brand name is spelled by the collision of sound and object, so the viewer's brain fuses the logo and the audio permanently.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** It actually reads slightly cheap today — and the *reason* is instructive: too many simultaneous whooshes, chrome-for-chrome's-sake, zero negative space or silence. It's memorable through repetition and sync-precision, not taste. Steal the sync discipline; leave the chrome maximalism.

Sources: [AVID — THQ](https://www.avid.wiki/THQ), [Closing Logo Group — THQ](https://closinglogogroup.fandom.com/wiki/THQ).

### 3. id Software (Quake/DOOM era)

**What it is.** Two generations. Quake II era: the "id" box logo on rusted brown steel gets *punched into the metal* piece by piece — top of the "i", bottom of the "d", the rest, the corner — like industrial stamping. Quake III: Team Arena / DOOM 3 / Quake 4 era: the logo reduces to a **white "id" box on pure black**, nearly static.

- **(a) Duration / structure:** Quake II version ~4–5s, 4–5 stamp beats in quick series then hold. DOOM 3 era: ~3s, ONE beat — appear, hold, gone.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Quake II era: each stamp is a percussive metallic impact, sound and dent simultaneous. Late era: essentially silent or a single low tone — the mark carries everything.
- **(c) The one thing:** Starkness as brand. id's ident *is* the reduction: as their engine tech got more impressive, the logo got quieter. White-on-black says "we don't have to show off; you already know."
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** The minimalist card is the confident-expensive pole (same family as Rockstar below). The stamping version demonstrates the other useful trick: a logo being *forged/struck into existence* in discrete percussive steps rather than fading in.

Sources: [AVID — id Software](https://www.avid.wiki/Id_Software), [Doom Wiki — id Software](https://doom.fandom.com/wiki/Id_Software).

### 4. Tony Hawk's Pro Skater intros (the opposite pole)

**What it is.** Not a logo sting — an anthem-driven hype montage. THPS1 cut real skate footage + in-engine footage to Goldfinger's "Superman"; THPS2 to Rage Against the Machine's "Guerrilla Radio". Real tricks, real *bails*, whip pans, punk-zine title cards.

- **(a) Duration / structure:** 60–90s. Structure = the song's structure: intro riff (slow establishing shots) → verse (rhythmic cutting, one clip per bar) → chorus (cut rate doubles, biggest airs) → breakdown (the bails/slams for comedy) → final chorus (title lockup).
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Cuts land on the backbeat; trick *impacts* (landings, slams) land on snare hits and riff accents. The montage never explains — it accumulates. Crucially the *energy curve of the edit follows the energy curve of the song*, not the other way around.
- **(c) The one thing:** Anthem-as-hype. The licensed track isn't background — it IS the identity (Kerrang's oral history: the soundtrack "became the soundtrack to players' lives"). Music sync is the montage's grammar: density of cuts = energy of song section.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive because the footage is real and the cutting is disciplined (one idea per shot, cut ON beat). Cheap versions of this grammar cut *near* the beat and pad with crossfades. Relevance to JAKESJAM: this is the correct grammar for the **build/high/peak** phases of a track with an actual drop — ceremony (Valve) for the quiet, hype-rhythm (THPS) once the track is moving.

Sources: [Kerrang — THPS soundtrack oral history](https://www.kerrang.com/20-years-of-tony-hawks-pro-skater-an-oral-history-of-the-soundtrack-with-tony-hawk-goldfingers-john-feldmann), [Consequence — Superman/THPS](https://consequence.net/2023/01/tony-hawk-goldfinger-superman-watch/).

### 5. EA "Challenge Everything" sting (2002–2006)

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~4s, three beats: logo zooms up from bottom → **presses itself flat with a flash** → slogan appears in the light ray below, shut-off sound.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Layered voices say "EA GAMES" as the logo arrives; a synth note with an audible *click* lands exactly on the press/flash; THEN — after the visual has settled — a boy **whispers** "challenge everything" over a now-still frame. Loud-hit-then-whisper: the voice line is placed *after* the impact, over stability, so it's intelligible and intimate.
- **(c) The one thing:** The stamp + whisper contour. Maximum-energy visual event, then a near-silent human voice. The contrast is the memorability.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive: only three events in four seconds; the whisper works because everything else stops moving. Also note EA's per-title variants (Def Jam's D-Mob voicing the line, MoH's 1940s film-grain treatment) — the *structure* stayed fixed while the skin flexed.

Sources: [AVID — Electronic Arts](https://www.avid.wiki/Electronic_Arts), [Closing Logo Group — Electronic Arts](https://closinglogogroup.fandom.com/wiki/Electronic_Arts).

### 6. Blizzard (glint-and-forge)

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~8–10s, two movements: (1) light/energy gathers — wavy light lines drift and slowly converge; (2) they resolve INTO the trembling-glyph wordmark, "ENTERTAINMENT" shines in beneath. The WoW-trailer variant is even purer: **white lines trace the logo's outline for ~6 seconds**, then the traced construction snaps to the finished logo.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Crackling/energy sounds while the lights gather, a whoosh on the reveal, quiet twinkling on the shine. Long gather → one release.
- **(c) The one thing:** The logo is *forged in front of you* — light becomes matter. The trace-then-snap variant is the closest existing ident to "compass-and-straightedge geometry constructs itself."
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive because the gathering phase is slow and asymmetric (feels physical, like weather), and because the payoff is withheld ~70% of the runtime. Cheap trace-reveals move at constant speed with uniform glow; Blizzard's has turbulence and acceleration.

Sources: [AVID — Blizzard Entertainment](https://www.avid.wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment), [Closing Logos — Blizzard](https://www.closinglogos.com/page/Blizzard_Entertainment), [logos-world — Blizzard](https://logos-world.net/blizzard-logo/).

### 7. Bethesda Game Studios (Skyrim card)

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~6–8s, ONE beat: metallic logo already on screen, slight continuous zoom, a single light sweep passes through it. Fallout-era variant swaps skin ("PLEASE STAND BY" test card → cog → static) but keeps the single-event shape.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** "Quiet dark ambiance" only (AVID's own description) — the light sweep is *not* scored. It flows directly into the main-menu drone, so the ident feels like the first note of the game rather than an ad before it.
- **(c) The one thing:** The light sweep across an otherwise inert metallic object — one specular event on one material. Materials do the branding.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive = physically-plausible material response (metal, engraved depth, believable specular). Cheap = a "shine" layer sliding over flat vector art. For JAKESJAM: this is the argument for the **bone-engraved** serif treatment reading as carved material with a real highlight pass, not flat text with a gradient wipe.

Sources: [AVID — Bethesda Game Studios](https://www.avid.wiki/Bethesda_Game_Studios), [logos-world — Bethesda](https://logos-world.net/bethesda-logo/).

### 8. Rockstar Games (the static card)

- **(a) Duration / structure:** 1 beat, indefinite hold. The R★ badge, centered, black or white field. In GTA loading flows it survives *as a still* through glitch transitions around it.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Frequently nothing at all; in loading sequences, ambient chatter/camera-shutter sounds and a synth whistle happen AROUND the mark while the mark itself never moves.
- **(c) The one thing:** Refusal to animate. Unchanged since 1998 (Jeremy Blake / Karen Scott design), through every GTA and RDR. The stillness reads as "we are the establishment."
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** The proof that expensiveness is a property of *confidence density*, not motion. A static card after motion is a power move; a static card instead of craft is just cheap. It works for Rockstar because the badge itself is impeccable — which is the bar the JAKESJAM lockup has to clear before it earns a freeze-frame ending.

Sources: [AVID — Rockstar Games](https://www.avid.wiki/Rockstar_Games), [designyourway — Rockstar logo](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/rockstar-games-logo/), [logome.ai — Rockstar](https://www.logome.ai/blogs/rockstar-games-logo).

### 9. PlayStation One boot (Takafumi Fujisawa, 1994) — the gold standard

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~17s, **two movements** with a hard join: (1) Sony Computer Entertainment diamond — deep bass swell + shimmering high texture, harmonically *unresolved*, ~10s; (2) cut to the PS logo — a settled, consonant chord with orchestral richness, ~7s. Mystery → affirmation.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Each screen owns one sound-world; the transition between logos IS the musical event. Fujisawa's stated design: start quietly "to avoid scaring users," then move from "a sense of security… to excitement"; start in equal temperament and drift to pure intonation so the listener "starts off stable and then feels a little bit off." The boot was also *functional*: the swoosh loops if the disc fails to read — the ident doubles as a status report.
- **(c) The one thing:** The two-movement harmonic arc — tension chord into resolution chord. People describe the PS1 boot as "feared" because movement one is deliberately uncanny; the relief of movement two is what made it loved. He cut a version with a whispered "PlayStation" for being too much — restraint again.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive: harmonic design doing narrative work; every parameter (temperament, chord voicing, dynamics) has an articulated intent. Cheap: a boot sound that is just "a nice chord" with no arc.

Sources: [PlayStation Blog — Fujisawa interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2019/12/05/how-takafumi-fujisawa-created-the-original-playstations-startup-sound/), [Siliconera — Fujisawa](https://www.siliconera.com/playstation-sound-designer-takafumi-fujisawa-on-creating-the-iconic-startup-sound/), [SVG — PlayStation startup sounds](https://www.svg.com/1166714/how-takafumi-fujisawa-and-his-teams-created-the-classic-startup-sounds-for-playstation/).

### 10. Sega scream / "SE-GA" chant

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~2s. One beat. Logo appears; choir sings the name.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Total fusion — the audio *is* the logo. Yuji Naka spent **1/8 of Sonic 1's 4MB cartridge** on that one sample, cutting a Sonic breakdance animation to afford it. The company judged 2 seconds of audio branding worth more than game content.
- **(c) The one thing:** Say the name. A spoken/sung brand name at the moment the mark appears creates recall no visual can match.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive as a *bet* (the cartridge budget), executed with zero decoration. Lesson for JAKESJAM: the spoken "Jakes Jam — by Intrepid Development" line is the single highest-recall asset in the ident; the visuals' job at that moment is to get out of its way.

Sources: [SEGAbits — SE-GA voice cartridge space](https://segabits.com/blog/2012/09/12/today-reddit-learned-that-the-se-ga-voice-at-the-start-of-the-sonic-the-hedgehog-took-up-18-of-the-cartridge-space/), [Indieground — Sega logo history](https://indieground.net/blog/sega-logo-history-how-four-letters-became-a-game-design-system/).

### 11. Bungie / Destiny Tricorn

- **(a)/(b):** Destiny title cards present the Tricorn white-on-black or as a subtle glow behind mission text; sequence idents are minimal fades under score swells rather than hit-synced stings.
- **(c) The one thing:** A symbol designed for *feeling* over meaning. Creator Christopher Barrett (pitch art, ~2008): the shape suggested "symmetry, a person raising their hands in celebration, maybe even something religious," channeling Alien-poster iconography. Bungie withheld the official meaning for ~20 years — ambiguity generated theory-crafting (wolf muzzle, figure greeting the Traveler), which is engagement.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** A sigil that *implies* mythology without stating it feels expensive. Direct relevance: the JAKESJAM monad sigil should never be explained on screen — no caption, no "lore text." Let it be the thing players screenshot and argue about.

Sources: [TheGamePost — Barrett explains the Tricorn](https://thegamepost.com/former-bungie-director-finally-explains-destiny-2-tricorn-logo-means/), [1000logos — Destiny](https://1000logos.net/destiny-logo/).

### 12. A24 (production-house pole) — most relevant single reference

- **(a) Duration / structure:** ~5–8s, three beats: (1) kinetic geometric elements fly in (a white stripe splitting into RGB channels, horizontal/vertical bars), (2) **a circle and semicircle slide into place and thin lines fly in and DRAW the "A24"**, (3) a brief RGB flash, then settle to solid white. GrandArmy built the mark itself from "rectangles, triangles, and perfect circles" with deco/mid-century serif DNA.
- **(b) Audio↔visual:** Per-film scoring — the animation is retimed/reskinned to each film's tone (flowers for *Midsommar*, googly eyes for *EEAAO*) while the underlying construction sequence stays identical.
- **(c) The one thing:** **Construction as reveal.** The logo isn't shown — it's *drafted*, out of primitive geometry, in front of you. This is exactly the compass-and-straightedge grammar JAKESJAM wants: the audience watches ruler-and-compass moves resolve into a wordmark and experiences the "oh it was always going to be that" click.
- **(d) Expensive vs cheap:** Expensive: the geometry that draws the logo is the SAME geometry the logo is made of (the construction is honest). Cheap trace-on effects draw arbitrary strokes over a finished mark. Honest construction = premium; decorative stroking = cheap.

Sources: [GrandArmy — A24 identity](https://www.grandarmy.com/projects/a24), [AVID — A24](https://www.avid.wiki/A24), [designyourway — A24 logo](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/a24-logo/).

## Part 2 — Synthesis: the JAKESJAM ident grammar

### The five laws extracted

1. **Stillness banks tension; motion spends it** (Valve, Rockstar, Bethesda). Every second of near-stillness before the drop multiplies the drop's perceived force. The intro must be *almost* static — sub-threshold motion only (slow zoom, breathing opacity). And the ending must return to stillness: a held card reads as confidence; a fidgeting fade reads as apology.
2. **Land ON the hit or don't land at all** (THQ, EA, THPS). Any event within ±80ms of a musical hit must be moved exactly onto it. Events that can't own a hit should be continuous processes (drawing, drifting, rotating), never discrete pops between beats. Discrete = on-beat; continuous = between beats. No exceptions.
3. **Construct, don't display** (A24, Blizzard trace, id's stamping). The logo/sigil must be *built* by visible geometric operations — and the construction must be honest: the arcs and lines that draw it are the arcs and lines it is made of. Withhold the completed form for most of the runtime (Blizzard holds ~70%).
4. **Two movements: mystery → affirmation** (PS1). The ident needs a harmonic-narrative arc, not just an energy ramp: an unresolved, slightly uncanny first movement (construction in the dark, gold-on-obsidian) that *resolves* into a consonant second movement (the reveal, rays, name). The relief IS the brand warmth.
5. **The voice is the crown jewel — clear the stage for it** (Sega, EA whisper). A spoken name outperforms any visual for recall. When the line plays, the frame must be *stable*: logo locked, no flashes, only slow continuous motion (drift, rays). EA's grammar: impact first, voice after, over stillness. Never put a flash or shake inside the spoken line.

Supporting clauses: material honesty (Bethesda — the bone-engraved serif needs a real specular sweep, carved depth, not a gradient wipe); unexplained sigil (Destiny — the monad gets no caption, ever); one accent color used as *causality* (teal appears only where energy currently is: the drawing tip, the hit-flash core, the ray source — never as passive decoration).

### Energy-grammar note

The researched idents split into **ceremony** (Valve, PS1, Blizzard, Bethesda — slow, withheld, single-payoff) and **hype** (THPS, THQ, Sega — beat-locked, dense, kinetic). A 27.9s track with a 4.8s drop demands BOTH, joined at the drop: ceremony grammar for 0–4.8s, hype grammar (cut/event density tracking musical density, THPS-style) from 4.8–19s, then a *return* to ceremony for the name and resolve (19–27.9s). That crossfade of grammars is the whole design.

### Phase-by-phase recommendation (27.9s)

**PHASE 0 — "The point" (0 → 4.8s) — ceremony. Valve stillness + PS1 movement-one uncanny.**
- 0.0s: hard black. Nothing. (Half a second of true black is itself a Valve move — patience as the first brand signal.)
- 0.6s: a single bone-ivory point — the monad seed — fades in at center over ~0.8s. Sub-threshold slow zoom-in begins (~2% over the whole phase) and never stops until 26s: the entire ident lives on one continuous slow push.
- 1.8–4.3s: faint dim-gold radial ticks breathe in at very low opacity around the point, irregular/asymmetric (Blizzard's turbulence rule — symmetry too early feels cheap). Optional: a hairline arc *begins* to inscribe at ~3.8s and is deliberately left incomplete — anticipation.
- 4.5–4.8s: everything dims 20% (the pre-drop "inhale" — energy withdrawal right before a hit is what makes the hit read).
- WHY: laws 1 and 4. This phase's only job is to make 4.8s feel inevitable. NO teal yet. Teal before the drop would spend the accent before it means anything.

**THE DROP (4.8s) — one frame, one truth. THQ/EA sync law.**
- ON the hit, within one frame: white-gold FLASH with a teal core, screen shake, particle burst — and the first great circle appears **fully drawn** (struck into existence like id's stamp, not eased in). The monad point is now inscribed.
- WHY: law 2 at maximum strictness. The drop is a discrete event; it must be instantaneous. The circle appearing complete-in-one-frame (vs animating on) is what separates "struck" from "rendered."

**PHASE 1 — "The construction" (5 → 14s) — hype grammar. A24 construction + Blizzard forge + THPS beat-density.**
- Compass-and-straightedge drawing, one operation per musical bar: arcs sweep to find intersections; chords connect them; the inscribed crystal diamond is cut **facet by facet, each edge landing on a beat**; radial tick groups stamp in on sub-hits (id-style, with micro-flash + 1–2px micro-shake per stamp).
- Teal exists ONLY as the moving pen-tip / active drawing edge; strokes cool to dim gold within ~400ms of being drawn (energy → matter, the Blizzard forge idea in color).
- Construction lines that are scaffolding (arcs used to find a point) fade to ~15% after use — honest drafting leaves ghost marks; it does not delete its work.
- Event density tracks musical density (THPS law): if the track adds a layer at ~9–10s, add a second concurrent drawing pen there.
- WHY: law 3. This is the longest phase because withholding the finished sigil is the tension engine (Blizzard's ~70% withhold ratio: sigil completes at ~14s ≈ 50%, lockup at 19s ≈ 68% — on target).

**PHASE 2 — "Ignition" (14 → 19s) — escalation hinge.**
- ON the 14s hit: the inscribed crystal diamond SNAPS into final registration with the circle (THQ red-bar thud moment) — flash, shake, particle ring.
- 14–19s: construction accelerates past human drafting speed (multiple pens, arcs completing in half-bars); the full monad sigil assembles; slow rotation of the whole geometry begins; god-rays fade in from behind the sigil, low intensity, raking; particles begin drifting upward through the rays.
- 18.3–19s: second pre-hit inhale — pens finish, drawing STOPS, rays dip. One breath of stillness before the biggest event (same trick as 4.5s; the grammar repeating teaches the audience the language).
- WHY: laws 2 + 3. The phase is a controlled loss of control — the geometry starts to exceed the draftsman, which is the emotional pivot from "made thing" to "revealed thing" (Destiny's feeling-over-meaning).

**PHASE 3 — "The name" (19 → 26s) — return to ceremony. EA stamp + Sega voice + Bethesda material.**
- ON the 19s hit: the bone-engraved serif **JAKESJAM** lockup stamps in — biggest flash + shake of the piece, geometry recoils/scales back to become the backdrop halo behind the type. Struck in one frame, like the drop.
- 19.5–20.5s: one Bethesda specular sweep travels across the engraved type — the only "material" event, unrepeated. God-rays at maximum; particles drifting; everything else now CONTINUOUS motion only.
- Spoken line placement rule (exact timing measured separately): "**Jakes Jam**" should sit just after the 19s stamp settles (≈19.8–21.5s), over a stabilized frame — EA's impact-then-voice contour. "**by Intrepid Development**" lands ≈23–24.5s, accompanied by a small dim-gold secondary text line fading in under the lockup (fade, not stamp — the sub-brand whispers, per EA's whisper law). NO flash, NO shake, NO discrete event anywhere inside either vocal phrase (law 5).
- If the measured vocal timing conflicts with this, move the *visual* events, never the voice: re-slot the specular sweep into whatever gap the vocal leaves.
- WHY: laws 4 + 5. This is PS1 movement two — consonance, warmth, the name. The frame must feel *resolved* while still being at peak musical energy: achieved by making all motion continuous (rays, drift, rotation) and zero motion discrete.

**PHASE 4 — "The seal" (26 → 27.9s) — Rockstar ending.**
- 26s: teal drains out of the frame over ~0.7s (the energy leaves; the mark remains). God-rays decay; particles fall away; rotation eases to zero.
- 26.7–27.9s: static card — JAKESJAM lockup + monad sigil in bone-ivory and dim gold on obsidian, the slow zoom finally easing to a stop. Hold the stillness. Cut (not fade) to menu at 27.9s, ideally with the menu inheriting the sigil position so the ident lands *inside* the game (Bethesda's ident-flows-into-menu trick).
- WHY: law 1's second half. After 21 seconds of construction and impact, the frozen card is the confidence statement. The teal drain enforces the accent-as-causality rule to the very end: when nothing is happening, there is no teal.

### Cheap-tells checklist (kill on sight)

- Any discrete event 50–200ms *after* its hit (the #1 amateur tell — THQ/EA got this right in 1998).
- The sigil or lockup fading in instead of being struck/drawn.
- Teal present anywhere energy isn't currently flowing.
- Decorative stroke-reveals that don't match the geometry's actual construction (dishonest drafting — A24 law).
- Any flash, stamp, or shake during the spoken line.
- Constant-speed, perfectly symmetric animation in the first 4.8s (turbulence and asymmetry read organic/expensive).
- Ending on a slow fade instead of a held card + cut.

### II.5 Verification flags & corrections (carry these — folk versions circulate)

- **PS2 towers** = per-title launch/play history from the memory card, NOT save files (GameSpot/TCRF). The folk version is close but wrong.
- **GameCube boot composer** = Toru **Minegishi**, not Totaka (Nintendo Power #204). Totaka is the *Wii channel music*.
- **Dreamcast startup** = Ryuichi Sakamoto — verified via Naganuma; PAL blue swirl = Tivola trademark conflict — verified.
- **Switch**: the console's normal boot is silent; the click lives in marketing + first-time Joy-Con attach. Click's sound designer publicly unattributed. Same for the **PS5** boot composer (SIE internal) — don't invent names.
- **THX**: "20,000 lines of C" was Moorer's own overstatement (~355 lines + patch); the sanctioned remake is the 2015 **Eclipse** (no "2018 recreation"; a "Dr. Dre era" framing doesn't check out).
- **HBO static angel** is early-90s and separate from the 1983 city open.
- **Netflix**: knock (wedding ring on cabinet) + *reversed* guitar bloom — it's a knock, not a plucked stinger.
- **Supergiant** logo = tiny figure before a rising star (not a torch). **Team Cherry** = cherry + controller buttons (no three-heads mark). **Stardew** boot audio = three mouse clicks (not an 8-bit chime).
- **Sega**: TWO artifacts — the Japanese sung chant (1983 SG-1000 ads → Sonic ROM) and the US screamed "SEGA!" (Matison, GBS ads 1992–96). The movies use the chant. Matison's "ill that day" detail: unverified.
- Approximate (not source-pinned) durations: Dreamcast, Steam Deck.

### New principles not captured in the first 5

Numbering continues from the five laws in Part 2.

6. **Idents are latency laundering** (N64 vs the CD consoles; Series X; Switch). The genre exists because disc seek time had to be filled; cartridge instant-on produced no ident at all, and as SSDs killed the dead time, idents shrank back toward silence. *A boot ident that adds delay must earn the delay it adds* — which is why the 27.9s JAKESJAM ident needs a skip affordance and/or first-boot gating (see 7).
7. **Budget for the 10,000th viewing, not the first** (Kojima gates his 2-minute film to first boot only; ConcernedApe hides a literal 10,000-boot message + 2% variants; TLOU boots straight to menu). Ceremony once, economy always: price ident length against forced repetition. JAKESJAM: full 27.9s on first boot; subsequent boots skip-on-input; consider a rare variant as folklore fuel (see 12).
8. **The ident as mirror** (PS2 towers; Steam Deck user movies). The strongest emotional variant of construct-don't-display is when what's constructed encodes *the player's own history* — and nobody is told. Discovery decades later generated a second wave of brand love for free. JAKESJAM candidate: a subtle parameter of the geometry (tick count, arc seed) derived from play data, never explained.
9. **Sound as telemetry** (PS1 swoosh loops on disc-read failure; PS3's sparkle only plays if boot succeeded). The premium idents are instruments, not decorations — the chime is a truthful status report, which is why it feels trustworthy. JAKESJAM: the resolve chord should be gated on "game actually ready," never faked.
10. **Ground the sound in a body-memory gesture** (PS3 orchestra tuning = imminence; X360 inhale; Switch latch snap; Netflix wedding-ring knock; HBO static-into-choir; Stardew mouse clicks). The idents that age best borrow an acoustic event humans already have a body-memory for; synthesized abstractions get replaced. Corollary: the source can be domestic and cheap — the budget goes into the halo around it.
11. **Generate the distribution, curate the take** (THX Deep Note: an RNG program whose shipped sound is one lucky run a human chose; the Sega chant was an imported 8-year-old ad jingle; the US scream was an on-set accident). Design the space of performances, then A&R the output — provenance and selection beat composed-to-spec.
12. **Hidden variants convert the boot into folklore — and scarcity makes it social** (GameCube's four-controller kabuki egg physically requires friends; PS2 tower myth-busting; Stardew's 2% Forest Magic). An ident with a secret has a second life as a story players tell each other.
13. **Audio is the continuity layer; visuals are the fashion layer** (Goldsmith's fanfare survived Universal's Weta redesign; "As Time Goes By" survived Pentagram's flattening; Moorer re-synthesized his own Deep Note; Neo Geo kept one melody through ~12 per-game timbres). Redesign the picture; re-*perform* the sound. Lock the JAKESJAM track and spoken line first — they must outlive any future visual reskin.
14. **Re-skin, never re-draw** (Blizzard's ice/gold/blood/static materials on one letterform; WB's per-film shields; Naughty Dog and Devolver per-game skins; generalizes Canon I's EA note). Hold structure + timing grammar invariant; flex only material and palette per context. One skeleton, many shaders.
15. **Slam on completion, texture the construction** (Blizzard's 30-year audio grammar: unpitched noise while forming → single slam ON legibility → twinkle decay; Capcom's glissando into the shine; Neo Geo's palette flash on the jingle's final beat). This *refines Law 2*: pre-hit audio should be unpitched texture so the one pitched/percussive event is reserved for the moment the mark becomes legible. Also the cheapest honest construct-reveal: Neo Geo's SNK logo is on-screen the whole time — revealed by a state change, not an animation.
16. **Permanent silence is a positioning choice, and it compounds** (FromSoftware since 1997; Annapurna; Team Cherry; Devolver's deadpan card). Distinct from Law 1 (a beat inside a sequence): this is *austerity as brand* — the mute card donates the first sonic moment to the product, and once reputation exists it reads as confidence. Earned, not chosen — a debut studio can't start here (see also Namco: bank recognition mute, earn a voice late).
17. **Diegetic handoff — the logo as the first object inside the fiction** (Uncharted 2's logo frozen under wind-blown snow; Diablo III's blood on cloth; GameCube's logo built from the console's own cube; extends Canon I's Bethesda menu-flow clause). Render the ident in the product's own material so it enters the world rather than preceding it. Marvel's self-canonization is the corporate-scale version: the ident's raw material graduating from borrowed authority to the studio's own output.
18. **The boot is a brag surface — and how you render it is a claim** (Neo Geo's "MAX 330 MEGA / 100MEGA SHOCK" spec-sheet-as-theater; OG Xbox rendering its reactor live on the GPU every boot = proof-of-power, vs PS5 shipping a pre-rendered movie = "we're a media brand now"; HBO airing a 10-minute making-of about its 90-second bumper). The ident can carry a claim, not just a mark — and rendering it real-time in-engine IS the claim for a game studio.
19. **Duration is platform economics, not aesthetics** (Netflix 3s for phone-speaker bingers vs Zimmer's 16s theatrical; THX's 30/45/60 cuts; Marvel's short/expanded versions; attract mode's infinite loop where every beat must sell independently because the viewer arrives mid-loop). Same mark, elastic time: retime per attention context rather than shipping one length everywhere.
20. **The mark can be a gesture or a character's act** (Riot's fist-bump — an action performed toward the audience, re-illustratable forever; Spider-Man webbing Insomniac's crescent back into place; Uka Uka vouching for Naughty Dog). A logo that *does* something — or has something done to it by the world's hero — transfers affection from character to studio. Riot is also the cautionary tale: a motion mark with no locked sonic signature.

## Stem channels — per-instrument audio-reactivity (2026-07-17)

The ident's reactivity runs on **per-stem envelopes** when `client/public/audio/splash-theme-stems.json` is present: 60Hz 0-255 envelopes + onset frame indices from a 6-stem separation of the anthem, indexed by `Math.floor(splashTheme.currentTime * fps)` — the same audio clock the WAAPI resync and shader `progress` already slave to (one clock, always). If the JSON is missing or fails to load, the live-FFT analyser path (mixed-track band guessing) drives the identical uniforms — the stems replace the *guessing*, never the grammar. `window.__identStems` reports `"live"` or `"fallback"`.

**The grammar holds: hits = springs, beds = envelopes.** Onsets kick the same underdamped springs (7.5Hz, ζ≈0.3, 130ms kick refractory); envelopes drive sustained levels. And "spark, not flood" still applies at REST — silence stays near-dark; the stems buy dynamic *range*, not a raised floor.

| Stem | Envelope drives | Onsets drive |
|---|---|---|
| **drums** | ring excursion / aperture / quake / glow (`uBass`) | the kick spring (`uKick`) — sample-accurate, replaces the FFT rising-edge detector |
| **bass** | **the wisp/melting-light bed (`uWisp`) — the headline channel.** Brightness (~10x range: near-invisible in bass silence → dominant molten at peaks), strand reach, drift speed (integrated clock `uWispT`), and chromatic fringe width ALL ride this envelope. The envelope IS the light. | — |
| **guitar + piano** (summed, clamped — piano is nearly silent in this track) | lead presence (`uLead`): crystal facet glint, ring highlights, seal saturation, pulse gate | the stab spring (`uStab`) — crystal diamond punches (guitar onsets) |
| **vocals** | SVG rays group brightness (the crown sings with the voice) | — |
| **other** | chroma width / saturation swell (`uChroma`) — composed INSIDE the existing growth escalation, growth stays the macro arc | — |

`air`/`scream` (shimmer + whiteout) stay FFT-derived in both modes — they're timbral registers of the whole mix, not separable instruments.

**Pipeline / regenerating.** `extract-envelopes.ts` (session scratchpad, 2026-07-17): htdemucs 6-stem WAVs → ffmpeg mono f32 → 1/60s RMS windows → `pow(v, 0.6)` perceptual curve → per-stem peak-normalize → 0-255 quantize; onsets = half-wave-rectified envelope delta ≥ 35% of that stem's max delta, 130ms refractory. Real DAW stems can replace the separated ones at any time: drop WAVs in, re-run extraction — same JSON shape (`{ fps, durationMs, stems: {drums,bass,guitar,piano,vocals,other}, onsets: {…} }`), no code change. Loader/consumer: `client/src/shell/identShader.ts` (`loadIdentStems`, `IdentBands` stem fields) + the ident block in `client/src/main.ts`.
