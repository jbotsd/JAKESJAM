Who we are

JAKESJAM is made by Jake Colson, trading as Intrepid Development, based in Perth, Western Australia. Under privacy law, Intrepid Development is the data controller — the party responsible for the data below.

What we collect

Everything below ties to a specific game feature. Nothing here is collected just to have it.

1. Your email address

When you first open the game, a screen offers to get you playing in about eight seconds if you give us your email. It's skippable. You can play the whole game, cosmetics included, without ever giving us one.

If you do submit it, you get one email a week: the Friday Fight Night invite. No other mail, no newsletter. We don't hand the list to anyone else for their own marketing. The address is stored in our database (Convex) and a local backup file on our own server.

2. Your callsign (in-game nickname)

A name you type in, up to 14 characters. Disallowed characters are filtered out. It's stored in your browser and sent to the game server so other players in your match can see it. It doesn't need to be your real name.

3. An anonymous player ID

The first time you play, your browser generates a random ID (like player_4k9x2z1p) and stores it locally. It's how the game recognizes you across a session and how purchased cosmetics stay attached to you. It isn't derived from your email or IP address. We can only connect it to you if you also gave us your email from that same browser.

4. Purchase data

JAKESJAM sells a few purely visual cosmetics (a $3 wand-trail recolor, for instance) that don't affect gameplay. Payment runs through Stripe's own hosted checkout — we don't handle or store your card details. Stripe sends us a confirmation, and we store your player ID, the item purchased, and Stripe's transaction ID. That's what it takes to grant the item and reconcile the sale.

5. Gameplay highlight clips

The game can auto-capture short clips of your own kills or big moments. Screen and game audio only, never your microphone or webcam. An uploaded clip gets a public link (play.elyad.io/c/<id>) that anyone with the URL can view. We may use standout clips to promote the game, including on TikTok. Clips are tied to your callsign at the time, not your email.

6. Crash & performance diagnostics

When something breaks or runs slowly, the game sends itself a small diagnostic event: error messages and stack traces, your GPU's renderer string, screen size and pixel density, the quality tier picked for you, and whether you're on touch or desktop. That's how bugs get fixed without you writing a report. Each browser tab gets a random session ID that lives only for that tab — never stored, never linked across sessions.

This goes to our own server only. No Sentry, no PostHog, no third-party error tracker.

How we use it

  • Email — the weekly Fight Night invite, nothing else.
  • Callsign / player ID — running matches, scoreboards, reconnecting you to a match in progress, and remembering which cosmetics you own.
  • Purchase records — granting the item you paid for, and the bookkeeping Australian tax law requires for completed sales.
  • Clips — giving you a shareable link, and promoting the game with the ones we choose to feature.
  • Diagnostics — finding and fixing bugs and performance problems, especially on hardware we don't own ourselves.

Who it's shared with

We use a small set of infrastructure providers to run JAKESJAM. Each one sees only the slice of data its job requires, and each has its own privacy policy:

ProviderWhat forTheir policy
Stripe Processes cosmetic purchases and sees your payment details directly — we don't. stripe.com/privacy
Convex Hosts the database for signups and match records. convex.dev/legal/privacy
Fly.io Runs the live game server (Singapore region). fly.io/legal/privacy-policy
Vercel Hosts the game's static client files. vercel.com/legal/privacy-policy
Cloudflare Routes traffic to play.elyad.io. Sees network-level metadata, like your IP address, in transit. cloudflare.com/privacypolicy

If you're playing JAKESJAM through a portal like CrazyGames or Poki rather than directly at play.elyad.io, that portal's own privacy policy also applies to whatever their embed collects.

How long we keep it

  • Email signups — kept until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete them.
  • Purchase / entitlement records — kept so you don't lose what you paid for, and for the five years Australian tax law requires for transaction records.
  • Clips — stored under a fixed disk quota. Once it fills, the oldest clips are deleted to make room. You can ask us to remove a specific one early.
  • Diagnostics events — same idea: a hard size cap, oldest events dropped first.

Your rights

Email us. There's no dashboard — one developer handles requests by hand, usually within a few days. You can ask us to:

  • Tell you what data we hold about you
  • Correct it
  • Delete it — including your email, callsign history, and clips
  • Unsubscribe you from the Fight Night email — we delete the record rather than just muting it

Everyone gets the same rights, wherever you're playing from. For EU/UK players this covers the GDPR right of access, rectification, and erasure. For California players it covers the equivalent CCPA rights to know and delete. In Australia, a business this small falls under the small-business exemption in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) — we follow the Australian Privacy Principles anyway.

Children

JAKESJAM isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect an email address from anyone under 13 without a parent or guardian's involvement. The email gate is optional, so a child can play the full game — matches, cosmetics, clips — without ever giving one. If you're a parent and think your child gave us one anyway, contact us and we'll delete it.

Security

Traffic to JAKESJAM is encrypted in transit (HTTPS end to end). Stripe webhook payloads are cryptographically verified before we act on them, so a forged "payment succeeded" event can't grant a cosmetic. Our own admin dashboard sits behind a secret token and a session cookie that never touches regular players.

Where it's processed

The game server runs out of Singapore (Fly.io). The client is served from Vercel's edge network. Convex and Cloudflare run their own global infrastructure. Your data may be processed in a country other than the one you're playing from — we rely on each provider's own compliance posture, including standard contractual clauses where relevant.

Changes to this policy

This page changes when what JAKESJAM collects changes, and the date at the top moves with it. Anything significant — a new category of data, a new third party, a new use — also gets flagged somewhere visible in the game.
